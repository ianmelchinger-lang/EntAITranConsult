A manufacturer with plants on three continents has run a forecasting tool at one plant for five months. The tool produces numbers on schedule, and the plant's operations lead has already told group leadership when a second plant will get the same tool.

What hasn't reached that leadership, because nobody has framed it as news, is this: the planning team stopped trusting the tool's numbers months ago and has kept its own spreadsheet ever since. The person who maintains the underlying data has explained the mismatch more than once. Nobody has decided what the tool's numbers may be used to decide, or who gets to make that call. And a second use case, at a different plant, is already easier to fund than settling whether the first one should scale at all.
