Name the second-order measure

A second-order measure tracks real value that reaches the financial statements by a long route: better decisions, more managerial bandwidth, experienced staff who stay. Priced with invented arithmetic, it invites doubt. Left out, it understates what the initiative did. Your job is to decide which lines earn a labeled place on the metric sheet.

A manufacturer’s service and warranty operation is writing the metric sheet for its AI drafting assistant before any results arrive. The operation states its strategic exposure plainly: it depends on a small group of experienced warranty analysts who are hard to replace. The plan allows at most two second-order measures. Six lines have been proposed.

Sort each proposed line.

Hard indicator means it lands in a cost line or an operational figure the budget owner already reviews. Second-order: keep and label means the value travels a longer route, the line is tied to the stated exposure, an existing record can supply the reading, and nothing is priced with estimates. Off the sheet as written means one of those conditions fails.

Second pass: change the exposure

Suppose experienced analysts were easy to hire, and the operation’s real exposure lay elsewhere: dealer contracts that come up for renewal and are at risk when claim decisions are disputed. Nothing here is checked.
