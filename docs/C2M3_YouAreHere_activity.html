A proxy multinational manufacturer has spent a quarter collecting candidate AI initiatives from its plants, its supply-chain function, its quality organization, its service business, finance, and HR. The result sits on one spreadsheet tab: eleven or twelve lines, a sponsor's name beside each, a sentence of description, and here and there a benefit figure carried over intact from a vendor's slide. The order in the file is the order the submissions arrived. The steering group meets in a week and expects a sequence.

Each proposal describes its own value in its own terms, against no stated measure — one in units saved, one in hours returned, one in a risk avoided that nobody has priced — and none of them says what it would cost anyone beyond the team that would build it.
