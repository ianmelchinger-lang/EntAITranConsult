Read the objection

An objection from the middle of an organization is evidence. The people there built their expertise and standing on how the work runs today, so they often see what a change will break before anyone else does. Each objection identifies something specific: a fact nobody has given them, something they need that isn’t there, or something they can’t yet do. Your job is to read which.

A manufacturer’s quality organization is moving documentation teams at three plants to assistant-drafted supplier-nonconformance write-ups, the records of parts a supplier delivered outside specification. You asked five people from the middle of the organization what they expect will break. Each card gives the objection, their answer, and what you checked.

Sort each objection by what it identifies. Ask what would have to be true for this person to stop objecting.

Awareness: what they need already exists, and nobody has told them.

Support: something they need isn’t there yet. A rule, a step, a measure, time, or authority has to be decided or built.

Ability: what they need is there, and they can’t yet do the new work.

Second pass: answer everyone with one message

Executives often answer objections with more messaging or a firmer mandate. Compare that reading with the one you just made. Nothing here is checked.
