State the frame

A cost frame is the declared basis on which a model classifies an AI system’s running cost and decides what evidence of return to accept. Practitioners divide on which frame fits. A model that never states its frame has still chosen one, by accident, and cannot explain the choice when finance asks.

A manufacturer’s service function runs an assistant that drafts replies to warranty and service questions. The ongoing cost of running it is often called inference spend, and practitioners disagree about what kind of cost that is.

The capacity frame treats the spend like headcount and budgets it against the hiring it defers. The technology frame puts the spend on the technology line and governs it as one position in a portfolio of investments.

Nine items follow. Some are things a frame compares the spend with, some are kinds of evidence a frame accepts, and some are cost lines. Sort each by the frame that brings it into view.

A cost that the initiative itself causes belongs in the model under either frame.

Second pass: switch the stated frame

The service function’s case holds two pieces of evidence. A budgeted warranty-analyst hire has gone unrequested for two quarters. And the hours claim rests on one team observed for one week.

Choose the frame the model states, and read the same evidence under it. Neither frame is the right one, and nothing here is checked.
