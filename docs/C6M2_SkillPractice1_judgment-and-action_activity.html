Judgment and action

An authority grant is the permission a system holds to act: the tools it may call, the actions it may take, and how far it may go before a person steps in. The model underneath makes judgments. The grant decides what happens next, and governance attaches to the grant. Two systems built on the same model can carry very different accountability, because they are allowed to do different things.

A manufacturer runs two automated systems. One is a workflow in the service function’s customer-exception path. The other automates maintenance scheduling at its plants. The cards below break them into steps.

Judgment: the step produces a decision, and nothing outside the system changes.

Acts alone: the step changes something outside the system, such as a customer’s inbox or a record, and no person stands before the change.

Waits for a person: the step changes something outside the system only after a person approves.

Second pass: add an approval step

Suppose the service function changes the workflow. A service agent now reads each drafted reply, edits it if needed, and approves it. The reply is sent and the record updated only after that approval. The model and its three judgments stay exactly as they were. Nothing here is checked until you sort both cards.
