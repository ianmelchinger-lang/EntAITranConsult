Follow each copy back to its source

A single source of truth is one agreed, current, access-controlled version of a domain’s data that every consumer reads from. It doesn’t require one system. Data can live in several places and still have one governing copy per domain. The failure arrives quietly: a consumer reads a copy that lags the governing one, and the answer looks like every other answer, with no marker of its age. The test is arithmetic. Add the refresh intervals along the path from the governing copy to the copy a consumer reads, then compare the total with how far behind that consumer can afford to be.

A manufacturer keeps parts consumption history. Each plant’s manufacturing execution system is the governing copy, and the data steward has named it as such. Two other copies exist. A composite planning extract, built from three plant systems, is rebuilt once every 168 hours (one week). A nightly snapshot copies the extract once every 24 hours. Five consumers read the history, and each states how far behind the governing copy it can afford to be.

The path: plant system (governing copy), then the composite planning extract (rebuilt every 168 hours), then the nightly snapshot (taken every 24 hours).

A change at the plant system waits at every hop between the governing copy and the copy a consumer reads. For each consumer, enter the longest a change can go unseen, in hours, then choose a verdict.

Governing copy: reads the copy the steward named. Within tolerance: reads a copy whose lag fits what the consumer needs. Past tolerance: reads a copy that can lag further than the consumer can afford. No agreed copy: reads a copy that nobody agreed governs.
