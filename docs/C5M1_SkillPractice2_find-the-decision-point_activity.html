Find the decision point

An investment made under uncertainty carries more than its projected cash flows. It also carries the right, without the obligation, to expand, delay, or stop as information arrives. That right is strategic option value, and it lives in the decision points of a staged plan. A decision point needs two things: new information that bears on whether the investment pays, and a choice that is still open because the money has not been committed.

A manufacturer’s quality organization proposes the supplier-document reader in three stages. Stage one is a pilot at one plant for twelve months. Stage two extends the reader to three plants over the next twelve. Stage three extends it to all nine plants over the following twelve. Each stage’s money is committed only when that stage begins. Six events from the plan follow, in no particular order. Every figure on this page is illustrative.

Sort each event as a decision point, a milestone, or a locked-in commitment.

A decision point brings new information and leaves a choice open. A milestone brings no new information about whether the reader pays. A locked-in commitment brings information, but its money is already committed, so nothing is left to decide.

What it leaves out: strategic option value

The case carries these sentences as a labeled, qualitative category beside the base case. It does not price them: formal option pricing needs inputs such as volatility and probabilities, and a business setting rarely supplies them. The reviewer weighs the category and the base case separately.
