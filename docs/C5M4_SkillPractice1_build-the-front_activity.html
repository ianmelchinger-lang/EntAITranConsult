Build the front

A decision-ready case is a business case sized to the decision it requests, so that an approver can act on it in the time they have. It has two layers. A short front carries the decision, the return and timeline, and the conditions they depend on. A back layer holds the supporting detail, where any reader who wants to test a claim can find it. When you right-size a case, you choose where each finding sits.

An advisor has finished a forty-page analysis of an AI assistant that helps inspectors at a manufacturer's plants catch defects. An escaped defect is a faulty unit that passes inspection and leaves the plant. A pilot has run at one plant.

The investment committee will give the case a short slot. The advisor wants one decision from it: fund the first phase at two plants.

Below are ten findings from the analysis. Place each one in the front layer or the back layer, or cut it from the case.

The one-page front

Each claim on the front carries a label for its evidence maturity. A measured and attributable claim rests on a recorded result that traces to the initiative. A directional claim rests on a signal moving the right way, before anyone can attribute it to one cause. An assumption is a bet about the future, with a date when evidence will confirm or replace it.

    A reader gives equal weight to claims written in one voice. The labels keep the recorded finding from being discounted along with the bets.
