Set the boundary between two funding disciplines

Two funding disciplines govern different lanes of an AI portfolio. Experiment discipline funds cheap, short-cycle attempts, each tied to a named business objective, and lets fast results prune the portfolio. Case discipline requires a business case before money is spent. Funding a cheap experiment like a permanent commitment wastes time, and funding a permanent commitment like a cheap experiment wastes money.

Practitioners divide on where the line falls.

Some who chair AI-enablement firms argue that every expenditure should connect to a business case now. Today’s low cost of experimentation is a temporary, subsidized condition, they say, and discipline installed late arrives as retrenchment rather than planning.

Some chief AI officers in professional-services firms argue the opposite. When building the real thing takes days, proving a concept first is the wrong activity, and a return case for each experiment is the wrong unit. They would run many cheap, short experiments, each tied to a named business objective, and let fast feedback prune the portfolio.

Each side states its position on conditions. Those conditions mark the boundary.
