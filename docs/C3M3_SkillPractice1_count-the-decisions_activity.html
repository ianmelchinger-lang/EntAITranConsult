Count the decisions

Organizational readiness is a condition you can check from outside an organization: people, process, and oversight lined up well enough for AI-supported work to run on an ordinary day. It breaks down into four decisions, and an assessment inventories whether each one was made and by whom. Your job is to tell a decision from something bought, attended, felt, or declared.

A manufacturer’s documentation teams at three plants are moving to assistant-drafted supplier-nonconformance write-ups, the records of parts a supplier delivered outside specification. A person reviews each write-up before it reaches the quality file, the controlled record. The sponsoring executive wants to know whether the change can start next quarter. Below are eight observations from the sponsor’s packet and from a first walk through the plants.

Sort each observation by the decision it shows was made, or choose No decision shown. An observation counts toward a decision only when it shows someone did the deciding and shows the result in the work itself.

Work redesigned: the procedure itself now has the tool in it, with its steps rewritten.

People trained: the people who use the tool have shown they can do the new work.

Handoffs defined: what passes between a person and the system, in what form, and when the result becomes the record.

Judgment placed: someone decided which calls stay with a person.
