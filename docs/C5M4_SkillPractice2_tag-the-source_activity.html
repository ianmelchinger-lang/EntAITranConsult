A business case inherits two limits from work the organization has already finished. The readiness assessment sets what the organization can support today. The roadmap sets what can happen, and in what order. Readiness limits what the case may claim, and phasing limits when the case may claim it will arrive. A claim with no source beside it asks the reader to take it on trust.

An advisor is drafting the case for an AI assistant that helps inspectors at a manufacturer's plants catch defects. An escaped defect is a faulty unit that passes inspection and leaves the plant. The first phase covers two plants: the plant that ran the pilot, and a second plant.

The findings and phases below are settled. The financial model follows them.

What the case inherits

Readiness findings

  • Records. Inspection records are usable as they stand at the pilot plant. Other plants recorded theirs differently, and each needs reconciling before the tool can run there. The second plant is one of them.
  • Shift use. In the pilot, two of three shifts stopped using the tool in its second month.

Roadmap

  • Phase one, months 1 to 6. The pilot plant keeps running the tool. The second plant's records are reconciled in months 4 to 6, and the tool goes live there in month 7.
  • Phase two, months 7 to 12. Further plants begin, only if a review at month 6 shows phase one's benefit.
