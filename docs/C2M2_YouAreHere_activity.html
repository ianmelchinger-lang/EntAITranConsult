A multinational manufacturer spent a quarter asking every function where AI might help. Thirty-eight ideas came back. Once near-duplicates are removed, twelve distinct candidates remain on the list a steering group will review next week, and the group expects three of them to come out the other side as a funded shortlist.

Each entry arrived as a sentence or two, in whatever shape the function that wrote it happened to use. One names a prediction someone wants. One names a system rather than a problem. One is a complaint about a slow process with the word AI stapled to the end. Nobody told the functions what shape to write in, so nothing on the list is directly comparable to anything else on it.

The advisor pulling the list together has no stake in any single entry, and is the only person in the room without one. Somebody still has to say, before the meeting, which of the twelve deserve a real look and which don't.
