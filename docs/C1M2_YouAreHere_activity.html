A manufacturer with plants on three continents is preparing for a board session on AI investment. Ahead of it, the IT and data lead is asked how many AI systems the company runs. The number she can defend is eleven: the deployments with a contract, an owner, and a line in the architecture record. That's the number headed to the board.

What isn't headed to the board, because nobody has framed it as news, is everything the eleven leaves out. A supply-planning team has run its own briefing assistant every day for eleven months, never sanctioned, and four people read what it produces each week. The best estimate anyone has for tools actually in use across the company, sanctioned or not, is closer to forty. Nobody has written a sentence saying what should count as a capability here, or whether a tool one team picked up on its own belongs on the same list as one that went through procurement.
