Which axis put it there?

A low position means two different things depending on which axis produced it. Low benefit means the idea is worth little. High effort means it's valuable but hard. Each calls for a different response, and a blended score can't tell you which you're looking at.

A manufacturer's transformation team has scored six candidates. Benefit combines the size of the claimed benefit with confidence in the evidence behind it. Effort covers money, time, people, and difficulty. Both run from 1 to 5. A colleague has added a blended score that folds the two into one number.

Choose the response each candidate calls for. Treat 3.5 or more as high benefit and 2.5 or less as low. Treat an effort of 4 or more as high and 2 or less as low.

Second pass: new evidence behind one benefit score

Month-end close support scored low on confidence because its benefit arrived as a currency figure with no method, and nothing on file could check it. Now the finance partner points out that the system of record already times every step of the close. The benefit could be measured before and after launch, so confidence in the evidence rises from 1 to 5. Nothing here is checked.
