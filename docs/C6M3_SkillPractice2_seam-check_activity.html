Finding the seam

A recommendation can be checked only to the degree that a reader can trace each claim to a piece of evidence and see where the advisor added inference. The seam between the two has to stay visible. A sentence that fuses them is not more persuasive than one that separates them. It is unverifiable, which is a different thing, and a finding the reader cannot trace can only be accepted or rejected on the advisor’s authority.

An analyst has drafted the opening of a recommendation to an executive committee on extending an automated maintenance-scheduling system from two plants to seven. The draft is welcome news. Before it goes up, you are marking up each sentence.

The data shows: a record could contain this sentence today.

I concluded: the sentence is the advisor’s judgment or prediction, and no record could contain it today.

Does both: the sentence states data and a conclusion in one breath. Choose it, then name the assumption that joins them.
