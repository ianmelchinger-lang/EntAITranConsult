Place it and price it

Where a candidate sits is part of what the candidate is. Its deployment layer is the level of the enterprise where the agent lives, and its permission tier is the amount of build authority and control that placement demands. Together they set its cost and its speed, so two candidates of equal value can differ sharply in price. An opportunity framed without them has not been priced.

An advisor holds three candidates. Each card says what the agent does, how far its actions reach, how easily its worst mistake is undone, and what data it uses. Assume all three would return the same value.

Place each candidate in a layer, and name who builds it. Two questions settle a placement, and they are asked separately.

The data and the stakes set the lowest layer. Personal assistance fits a draft that one person reads before anything happens. The governed layer, where business-based builders assemble workflows inside a platform whose controls are already built in, fits an agent that reads work systems and runs by itself. The production layer, for agentic flows on core governed data with full observability, is required by anything that runs on core governed data or takes consequential action beyond its owner’s own work. Full observability means the organization can see what each flow reads, decides, and does. A candidate may sit above its lowest layer, at a higher price.

Reach and reversibility set who may build it. Reach is how far the agent’s actions extend. Reversibility is how easily someone can undo its mistakes. The owner may build alone when the agent’s reach ends with the owner and any mistake is undone by ignoring it. Otherwise the owner needs a build specialist.
