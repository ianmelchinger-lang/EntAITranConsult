Cut to the surface

The agentic surface is the set of steps inside a workflow that need interpretation or judgment. It is always smaller than the process around it, and the opportunity’s size, risk, and build path follow from it. The cut runs in a fixed order: remove what should not exist, leave rule-following work on ordinary automation, place agents only at the steps that need judgment, and keep a person alongside wherever a recommendation turns into an action.

A plant quality team handles supplier nonconformance, meaning a delivery that fails its specification, in eleven steps from a rejected lot to a closed file. Each card says who does the step, what depends on it, and whether it decides by one rule every time or by reading and weighing.

Give each step one label.

Remove means the step should not exist. Ordinary automation means one rule, run reliably. Agent means the step needs interpretation. Agent with a person alongside means the agent’s result turns into an action someone must answer for.
