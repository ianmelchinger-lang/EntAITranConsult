Discount by period

Present value is what a future benefit or cost is worth today. Money in hand can be put to work while you wait, so an amount that arrives later counts for less, and it counts for less with every year it waits. A case that adds present and future amounts at face value looks stronger than it is.

A manufacturer’s service function wants an assistant that drafts replies to warranty and service questions. Its case sets a payment at signing against three years of net benefit and one later fee. Each net benefit is what remains after running costs and the work the assistant moves to other teams.

The page adds all five amounts at face value. The total is +$36,000, and the page calls the case positive. Finance uses a discount rate of 9 percent, the yearly rate at which it marks down money it has to wait for. All amounts here are illustrative.

For each line, choose how long the amount waits before it arrives. Count an amount at the end of the year it arrives in. An amount paid at signing does not wait.
