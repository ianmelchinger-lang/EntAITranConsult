Trace the baseline

A baseline is a reading of a process, taken before an initiative arrives, by the same instrument and the same definition that will produce every later reading. Any claim that work got faster rests on it. Your job is to test each “before” figure against the reading that will come after.

A manufacturer’s service and warranty operation put an AI drafting assistant into its claims queue two quarters ago. Handling time now stands at 14.2 minutes per claim. The next review will read that figure from the workforce management system’s standard report, under the definition now in use, which includes the time agents spend on wrap-up notes. Five “before” figures are on the table.

Sort each figure by tracing three things: the system that produced it, the definition behind it, and the weeks it covers.

Use as the baseline means the same system, the same definition, and a period the assistant didn’t touch. Fails a check means a system in this operation produced it, but at least one of those three is off. Not a reading of this process means it came from asking people or from another organization.

A bucket may stay empty.
