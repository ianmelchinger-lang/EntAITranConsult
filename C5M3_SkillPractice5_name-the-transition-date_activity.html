Name the transition date

Evidence maturation is the change in the kind of proof an initiative can honestly offer as people adopt it: first observational, then comparative at team level, then financial. Demand the later kind too early, and honest silence reads as failure. Never demand it, and stories stand in for proof. Your job is to put both demands on the calendar.

A manufacturer’s service and warranty operation is about to give an AI drafting assistant to seven teams, and its measurement plan covers the first six quarters. Practitioners divide on when harder evidence should be demanded. Finance wants returns demanded early, because spending without demanded returns is how budgets bloat. The people leading adoption want the first phase protected, because early financial pressure ends the experiments that produce the value. Both accept that the demand should come.

  • Observational evidence is available from the first quarter: supervisors report specific changes in what their teams can do.
  • Comparative evidence can first exist once five of the seven teams have spent a full quarter above the adoption gate. Examples are a backfill left unrequested, or outside spend that falls for the same output. The plan requires fresh justification for every hire already budgeted, so work a team has absorbed shows up as a request not made.
  • Financial evidence, such as a closed cost line, reaches the books two quarters after comparative evidence first exists.
  • The review that decides funding for the next wave sits at the end of quarter 4.
